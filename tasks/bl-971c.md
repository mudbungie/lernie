Round-2 devadmin lane. yog's `/enroll` gained `--at <host>:<port>` (PROTOCOL 15,
yog bl-fec6), whose whole purpose is the case this lane is:

    `--at <host>:<port>` states the address THIS device will dial, for a device
    that does not share this engine's own view of itself — an emulator reaching
    its host through the emulator's alias, a phone on the LAN, a box on an
    overlay. Unstated is the engine's own `wire/address`.

The seat cannot pass it:

    $ lernie enroll ops alpha2 foot --at host.containers.internal:7773 --into <dir>
    lernie: `lernie enroll` takes one optional word after the three, and it is
    `--into <dir>` — not "--at host.containers.internal:7773 --into <dir>"
    `lernie help` lists every word; `lernie help <word>` is one word's page

## Why this is the whole enrolment, not a flag

The engine bound its LAN address and `wire/address` says so. A rootless podman
container cannot reach the host's LAN address at all:

    $ podman exec <box> bash -c '</dev/tcp/<lan-ip>/7773'
    bash: connect: Connection refused

It reaches the same engine perfectly through `host.containers.internal`. So the
four files `lernie enroll --into` wrote were correct in every byte except the
`address`, and the foot that carried them printed nothing, dialled nothing and
waited. The failure is silent at both ends: the foot is up, the engine has the
client registered, `lernie clients` shows it not present, and nothing says why.

That silence is exactly what bl-fec6 was filed about, and the repair exists —
one word behind the seat's argument parser.

## Workaround used, and what it costs

    yog gesture --ws ops "/enroll alpha2 foot --at host.containers.internal:7773"

then jq the four fields out of the reply by hand. That works only for an
operator sitting ON the engine box. A seat on any other machine — which is what
a seat is — has `lernie enroll` and nothing else, so it can enrol a device only
onto the address the engine happens to have bound.

## Ask

`lernie enroll <workspace> <name> <grade> [--at <host>:<port>] [--into <dir>]`,
both optional and independent. The refusal above already enumerates what it
accepts, so it is one entry in that list and one field on the envelope.

p2: it is not a workaround-and-continue for anyone who is not on the engine's
own box, and the address it silently gets wrong is the one field that produces
no error message anywhere.
