Round-2 install lane, second-box scenario. lernie 0.1.33 (main tip 1763302) on a container that holds no engine of its own and is a seat for an engine on another box, provisioned exactly as `lernie enroll … --into` instructs: the entry directory carried into `<lernie-data>/wire/workspaces/home/`.

GESTURE: `lernie workspaces`

VERBATIM:

    (this box's own engine)
        no wire provisioned at <lernie-data>/wire: if an engine runs on this box its
        material already exists — its own boot minted it — so the act is a COPY: take
        `ca.pem`, `client.pem`, `client.key` and `address` out of that engine's wire
        directory ($XDG_DATA_HOME/yog/wire) and put them here, under those names. …
    home
        workspaces
          home  named

The roster is correct and the read WORKED — `home` is right there under it. But the box is asked about a channel it was never meant to have, and told a recipe that begins "if an engine runs on this box", on a box where none does and none is meant to. On a pure remote seat this paragraph is printed above every roster read, forever, and the first-time reading of it is "something is broken".

WHAT WAS EXPECTED: the bare same-box channel is a channel only when it is provisioned. An unprovisioned `<lernie-data>/wire/` on a box that holds named channels is an absence, not a fault — say nothing, or say it once at the bottom as a note rather than at the top as a refusal.

NOTE THE SENTENCE ITSELF IS THE bl-ad7f FIX AND IT IS VERY GOOD — named files, named source directory, the `:0` re-issue remedy and the visiting-box case separated. bl-ad7f is FIXED and this is not a regression of it: the wording is what was asked for, and what is left is that the channel is enumerated at all.

Scoped: `lernie conversations home` does NOT carry it, so it is the roster read specifically.

SEVERITY p3: nothing fails, and the noise is one paragraph. It costs a first-time user confidence at the exact moment they are checking whether the carry worked.
