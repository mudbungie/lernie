On a box whose data root holds no flat `wire/` and two entries under `wire/workspaces/`, `lernie entries` lists both and the window attaches to both — and `lernie workspaces` answers:

    lernie: no wire provisioned at <data root>/wire: the pair is minted on the host that issued it (`yog wire-certs WIRE_LEAF=<name>` there) and carried here by hand; the seat mints nothing

The routing is REMOTE §8.2 working as written — *'a gesture naming no workspace goes where it always went, the flat directory's client material'* — and `workspaces` takes no argument, so it can never name one. But the same question asked two ways now gets two different answers on one box: the window's roster is *'the union across those slices, local first and then the entries in leaf order, every row carrying the channel it came from'*, while the CLI verb for that question sees one channel and refuses when that channel is absent.

The sharp case is the ordinary one for a seat: a laptop that holds no engine of its own and is a client of a server elsewhere. It has channels, it has workspaces, the window paints them — and the verb whose whole subject is 'every workspace' tells the operator nothing is provisioned. The escape hatch already answers (`lernie ask '{"op":"workspaces","workspace":"<leaf>"}'` returns that entry's rows), which is what makes this a shorthand gap rather than a missing capability.

What it probably wants is the window's own rule: fan the no-name query over every channel and print the union, each row stamped with the channel it came from — the same composition `entries` already walks, and no new flag. Whether the flat channel being absent should still be *said* (it is a real hole on a box that is supposed to hold one) is the part to decide.

Found while installing the seat on a laptop against a local engine (yog bl-17f8).

## Re-driven on 0.1.0: the missing flat channel is not the condition

The case above reproduces exactly — a scratch data root holding no flat `wire/`
and one entry under `wire/workspaces/` lists that entry in `lernie entries`,
paints its walls in the window's roster, and answers the same
`no wire provisioned at …` from `lernie workspaces`.

**But a box that HAS a flat channel shows the same hole, and that is the
ordinary box.** Driven against a box holding all three shapes — a flat channel,
one entry addressing another engine, one entry addressing the same engine under
a different trust root — the window's roster painted three sections and every
wall in each, while

    $ lernie workspaces

answered one channel's rows and nothing else. Nothing refused, nothing was
missing-looking, and nothing said the answer was partial: the verb whose whole
subject is *"every workspace this engine holds"* silently means *this box's own
engine* and the operator has no way to learn that from the output.

So the condition is not "no flat channel". It is that `workspaces` is the only
roster question with no way to say which channel it is about — the CLI's answer
is one channel's, the window's is the union, and the two are the same question.
The remedy this ball already proposes (fan the no-name query over every channel,
stamp each row with the channel it came from — the composition `entries` already
walks) covers both, and is the only one that makes the two surfaces agree.

The half-provisioned and hollow cases behave well and are not this: a missing
material file names the file, and an entry directory with nothing in it says it
is an empty entry.
