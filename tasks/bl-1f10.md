## What landed

**The act.** `enroll` is a row in the verb table like any other — three named strings, `workspace`, `name`, `grade` — so the window and the command line spend one serialization and the corpus's round-trip check pins the row against yog's own request fixture, field for field.

**Two faces, one gesture.** `lernie enroll <workspace> <name> <grade>` from argv, and a control on the aimed wall in the roster. The control hangs off the AIMED row and no other: the act mints the pair `(client, workspace)`, and a workspace is exactly what an aim is.

**The reply is not the product, and that is the one place this seat departs from its own shape.** Every other verb hands its reply stream to stdout. This one carries a private key for a box that does not exist yet, and stdout is a scrollback, a shell history and whatever it was piped into — so `enroll` has an arm of its own all the way up to the CLI's decision type, and what it prints is the picture plus the three fields that are not secret. `lernie ask` still prints the raw frame, which is correct: an operator who spells the envelope by hand asked for the stream, and nothing here is a boundary against the operator.

**Nothing is written down, and it is asserted over the TREE.** No file, no cache, no log line, no temporary anything, on either face. The suite drives the whole act against the stand-in engine over a throwaway root and walks that root before and after, comparing paths and sizes — over the tree rather than over the paths the code happens to know about, because a defect here is precisely a path nobody thought of. The window's half is a control whose entire product is a forgetting, and material that arrives for a pane the operator already closed is dropped **and said so**: the enrollment happened on the engine, and an operator who is not told goes looking for a box with a registration and no material.

## The QR encoder, which is this crate's own

`src/qr/`, seven files, byte mode at correction level M, the smallest version that fits — and **no new dependency**. A QR symbol is a fully specified algorithm rather than a research problem. Three tables the standard prints are derived instead: the block split is the even division of the data codewords and its remainder, the remainder-bit table is a table of zeroes dissolved by reading past the end of the bit stream as light, and there are no logarithm tables because the field multiply is the carry-less multiply itself. The one surviving exception is version 32's alignment row, which no formula generates and which is therefore named.

**Two things the standard leaves open, and both bit.** Building against TWO independent implementations was worth more than one, because they disagree, and where they disagree is where a lone oracle would have made this encoder wrong.

- **The pad codeword.** One of them appends a spurious zero codeword whenever the message plus its terminator lands on a byte boundary — which in byte mode is always. The standard adds padding bits only when the stream does not end at one. The symbol still decodes, so it is invisible, and it means that library can never be a byte-for-byte oracle. The other agrees with this encoder to the bit.
- **Penalty rule 3.** The standard prints two eleven-module patterns and also describes them in prose, and the two readings differ on a core with light on both sides. This follows the patterns as printed, which is the reading that leaves an oracle standing. Rule 3 only changes which mask wins and all eight decode.

Two orderings were defects and are now stated at the site: the timing lines go down **before** the finders overwrite their ends, and both error-corrected headers are written **after** the mask scoring — the penalties judge the masked data, and a header whose content is a function of the mask choice cannot also be an input to it.

## The QR's proven path

Act → material → matrix, with a real mint rather than a fixture. A P-256 pair minted by the suite's own `openssl` recipe, answered as an `enrolled` frame by the stand-in engine, read by the reply vocabulary, re-said as the envelope, and drawn:

- **1601 bytes** of compact JSON — the same order as REMOTE §8.4's 1567, the difference being subject names — in a **version 33** symbol, 149 modules, at level M. REMOTE's rule is *PEM as minted, at level M or lower*.
- The matrix is **byte-for-byte identical** to an independent implementation's for the same bytes as one byte-mode segment.
- A real camera-grade decoder reads the symbol back to the **exact** envelope bytes, at three rendering scales.

Verified out of band and reported rather than pinned, because the tools are not in the gate. What the suite pins is three whole matrices, all forty versions' side, mask and dark count, all thirty-nine alignment rows, and the standard's own published Reed-Solomon example, format table and version blocks.
