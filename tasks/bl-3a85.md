## Why now
'Role' is load-bearing across the codebase (worker, compactor) and appears ~everywhere in ARCH §4.3, §3.3, souls/, providers.yaml — but it is NOT defined as a lernie term of art in docs/TAXONOMY.md (only generic industry 'role' usage appears there). Per CLAUDE.md terminology discipline, a term of art in code/docs/prompts needs an explicit definition. The user notes the concept hasn't been talked through recently. This ball sets it down.

## Deliverable (living document, edited like code — not a growing task body)
A design doc capturing the settled nature of a role. Landing home to decide: a new §4.3 rewrite in docs/ARCHITECTURE.md plus a TAXONOMY.md entry, vs a standalone docs/ design note that ARCH/TAXONOMY reference. Recommend: the authoritative definition lands in TAXONOMY.md (it's a vocabulary fact) and §4.3 becomes its mechanism; the design rationale can be a tracked note. Confirm with user.

## What the design must pin (the questions to answer, not the answers)
Today a role is an entry in a config-commit's providers.yaml `roles:` map. From the shipped code, a role currently binds FOUR things to one name:
1. **A model** — (provider row, model id) pointer, cross-validated against models.yaml (§4.3).
2. **A toolset** — the `tools: [...]` list selecting from the on-disk tool pool (§3.3). Note: compactor's toolset (write_summary/mark_for_deletion) is NOT declared here — it's built into the compactor primitive (§2.7). That's an inconsistency worth naming: is a role's toolset always config, or can it be primitive-owned?
3. **A soul** — souls/<role>.md by convention, copied to the branch's soul.md at dispatch (§2.3 step 2). No path override; the name IS the path.
4. **(v1.1) A capability grant** — the fs/net/exec/clock/env ceiling (§4.3, §3.6), severability home for capability policy.

## The questions to settle
- **Is a role a noun or an adjective?** Is 'worker' a *kind of agent* (identity) or a *bundle of config knobs* (model+tools+soul+grant) that any dispatch selects? The dispatch primitive (bl-c33b) takes `role` as an input; the workflow appendix binds terminal→result-message regardless of role. Does role carry ANY behavior, or is it purely a config-selection key? (Lean: pure selection key — role is the name under which a config-commit bundles model+toolset+soul+grant. Confirm.)
- **Is the role set open or closed?** ARCH says roles 'slot in by name' (compactor/worker today, verifier tomorrow — the v0.7 success criterion is a verifier role running with zero code changes). If open, what makes a role valid — just the presence of souls/<role>.md + a roles.<name> entry? Is there any role the CODE knows by name (compactor's primitive toolset suggests yes — compactor is special)? Reconcile: are there 'primitive roles' (code knows them: compactor) vs 'config roles' (pure config: worker, verifier)? That two-tier split may be the actual answer, or may be a smell to dissolve.
- **Single source of truth for the role→X bindings.** Model is in providers.yaml + validated against models.yaml. Soul is derived from the name (souls/<role>.md). Toolset is in providers.yaml EXCEPT compactor's. Where does each binding authoritatively live, and does anything mirror? The compactor-toolset-in-primitive exception is the SSOT wrinkle to resolve.
- **Relationship to dispatch.** `lernie dispatch <role> --goal ...` (§2.5). The role is resolved against the governing config commit (nearest config/* ancestor, §2.2). A child's role vs its parent's role — independent? Can a worker dispatch a worker? A verifier? What constrains the role a dispatch may name?
- **Coordinate with bl-3157** (model-mismatch bug): that ball asks whether a role should name a concrete model id or a capability profile. If roles name capability profiles resolved from bz --list-models, that reshapes binding #1 here. These two balls must not settle the same question two ways — this design ball owns the role→model binding SHAPE; bl-3157 owns the concrete-id-validity mechanics.

## Attack-the-design prompt (per user's arch guidance)
What does 'role' NOT solve? If the honest answer is 'role is just a config-bundle name and carries no behavior', then maybe the term of art is thinner than the codebase's ceremony implies — and the taxonomy entry should say exactly that, to stop future code from over-loading it. Fill holes with a principle, not a feature.
