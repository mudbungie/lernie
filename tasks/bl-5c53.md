`config` and `lineages` are the two ops bl-4a2c left behind, and they are one
subject: a config file's BYTES, on a lineage, at a path.

The tuning quartet that landed there reads and writes one derived thing — the
`providers.yaml` role assignment, through four ops that each carry a role and a
value. This is the other half, and it is a different shape in every respect:

- `lineages` answers rows of `{name, oid, short_oid, committed, files[]}` —
  the policy branches a conversation is born on, each with its tip and every
  path that commit holds. It is the listing the file read then indexes into.
- `config` is ONE op that is a read and a write depending on whether the
  envelope carries `text` at all (upstream's own discriminator), and its
  destination is a nested `target` object with seven possible fields across
  five destination shapes: `brazen`, `litany-models`, `cadence`,
  `litany-workflow` + `name`, and a lineage under `branch`/`fork`/`orphan`
  with `lineage`, `path` and — for `fork` alone — `source`.

Two consequences for this seat. The gesture is a **nested object**, so it can
never be a row in `src/verbs/rows.rs` and must be a typed door beside them
(`src/verbs/start.rs` and `src/verbs/tuning.rs` are the two precedents). And
the pane is a text editor over arbitrary bytes with two pickers above it —
which is a different interaction from the tuning pane's seats and toggles, and
is why it is not a section of that pane.

`reply/config` answers `{text}`; a write answers a captured run, which this
seat already paints.

Exemption rows in `parity.toml` cite this ball until the surface lands.
