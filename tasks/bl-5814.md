Two comment blocks in `scripts/leak-rules.sh` were ported from the sibling repository and never re-aimed at this one. Both are prose beside a live pattern, so what they mis-describe is what the gate is doing.

- Above `FORBIDDEN_PATH`: *"THRALL'S OWN KEY MATERIAL IS ALREADY COVERED... A foot holds an operator-issued leaf and its key, carried to the box by hand (DESIGN §3.3)"*, and a forward reference to a ball id that is not this project's. A seat holds an operator-issued leaf too, and the shape rule covers it for the same reason — but the paragraph names the wrong component, cites a section number that means something else here, and points at a channel ball that has already landed under a different id.
- The pattern itself lists `.config/brazen`, which is the LLM adapter's directory. This crate links no adapter and never will (DESIGN §3: it never executes, it never mints). Whether that entry earns its place here is the question — a path rule costs nothing and refuses a real shape, so keeping it is defensible; carrying it unexamined is not.

The rule table is the ONE definition of what may not be committed, and its comments are the only place the reasoning lives. A reader who checks whether a pattern is right reads the paragraph above it, and here that paragraph is about another program.

Fix the prose to this repository's facts, rule on the `.config/brazen` entry either way, and leave the patterns themselves alone unless the ruling moves one.
