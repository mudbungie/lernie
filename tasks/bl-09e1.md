Gesture: /attention on a seat whose engine holds asking conversations (yog fixture busy, engine 0.0.77, PROTOCOL 19); also 'lernie attention' on the command line.
Expected: the queue's rows on the next beat; the CLI prints and exits in well under a second, as 'lernie workspaces' does (0.05 s).
Observed: the pane reads 'waiting to hear what is asking for you' for ~30 s, then the rows land; 'lernie attention' takes 30.2 s every time, with or without a window running. Cause: since PROTOCOL 10 (yog REMOTE 14.1, the attention lane) an engine intake that can hold answers attention as a SEQUENCE — the answer at connect, then a frame per change, until the follow lane's 30 s hold ends. Channel::ask collects every frame to the terminator, so every one-shot attention ask (offframe asker fan, seat::sent for the CLI, seat::fan) waits out the whole hold. In the window the asker's pass is one thread, so while the queue pane stands or the selected conversation asks (Standing::asking) every other standing read — roster, aimed wall, transcript — is also 30 s late per channel per pass.
Why it matters: the ask box above the prompt is fed by this read, so a conversation that starts asking is not answerable for up to 30 s per channel, and the whole window goes stale meanwhile. REMOTE 14.1: 'frames replace; they never append' and 'a seat paints the last frame it holds' — a one-shot ask is answered by its first frame.
