Round-1 daily lane. lernie 0.1.30 against yog 0.0.38.

`lernie help follow` and the verb table both promise "hold the line on one conversation's live tail". It returns 0 after a few seconds, mid-turn, every time.

Three consecutive runs against one conversation that was in flight throughout (`lernie agent` said `in-flight` before and after each):

    --- follow attempt 1 ---
    {"kind":"follow","ok":true,"stream":{"delta":"thinking"}}
      wall=2.90s
    --- follow attempt 2 ---
    {"kind":"follow","ok":true,"stream":{"delta":"thinking"}}
    {"kind":"follow","ok":true,"stream":{"delta":"thinking"}}
      wall=7.81s
    --- follow attempt 3 ---
    {"kind":"follow","ok":true,"stream":{"delta":"thinking"}}
    {"kind":"follow","ok":true,"stream":{"delta":"text","text":"Now I have a solid understanding of the repo. Let me write the REPORT.md."}}
      wall=6.00s

The engine is behaving as REMOTE §5.1 documents it — "a frame per growth of the open response.json and no terminator until the stream closes at the step boundary" — so the read ends at each STEP, not at the turn. The window's follow lane handles that: REMOTE says "re-ask is the whole reconnect ladder", and the lane re-asks. The CLI verb does not, and its own page describes the turn-shaped behaviour rather than the step-shaped one.

Consequence: watching an agent from a terminal is `while true; do lernie follow ...; done`, which the operator has to invent, and which loses whatever landed between two reads. A conversation this lane ran took 29 steps; that is 29 invocations to watch one goal.

A second, smaller thing on the same surface: a `thinking` delta arrives with no text at all — `{"delta":"thinking"}` and nothing else — so a follow of a reasoning-heavy stretch prints the same eight characters over and over and says nothing about progress.

Expected: `lernie follow` re-asks across step boundaries and returns when the conversation is no longer in flight, or takes a flag that says which it is doing. Either way the page and the behaviour agree.

Severity p2: the verb exists, answers ok, and does not do the one thing its name is.
