Round-2 devadmin lane. bl-1554 is fixed: `--into <dir>` writes the four files
and a headless foot can be enrolled. The closing sentence is good:

    filed as an entry in <dir>: ca.pem, client.pem, client.key, address. Carry
    the directory to the box it is for, under its own `wire/workspaces/<name>/`

## The defect

`--into` also prints, to stdout, on the way there:

  1. the 145-column QR symbol, and
  2. the whole `{"yog-enroll":1,…}` envelope as one line — which carries
     the `key` field, whole, as a PEM private key.

Then:

    the seat keeps no copy — scan the symbol or take the line; they are the same
    bytes. Enroll again if it is lost

## Why it matters more with `--into` than without

`lernie help enroll` states the audience: `--into <dir>` is "for a box with
neither" a camera nor a paste box. That operator is at a terminal, over ssh, in
a shell with history, probably inside a multiplexer with a scrollback buffer,
possibly in a CI log or an agent transcript. The one route designed for them is
the route that sprays the private key into all of it — while the last line
says the seat keeps nothing, which is true of the seat's own files and false of
the terminal it just wrote to.

Without `--into` the print is the point: the QR and the line ARE the delivery.
With `--into` the delivery is the directory, and the print is pure residue.

## Ask

With `--into`, print the receipt and not the material: the four filenames, the
directory, the name and the grade. The QR and the raw line stay exactly as they
are on the bare form, where they are the carrier.

If the two are wanted together for an operator who asked for both, that is a
second flag; the default for `--into` should be that the key reaches the
directory and nowhere else.

p2: no key is exposed to a third party by the seat itself, but the operator is
given no way to take the documented headless route without leaving a private key
in their scrollback, and this is the flow a first-time user runs.
