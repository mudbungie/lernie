## What is being asked for

The engine grows an act — an enroll, naming a box and a grade (`foot` or `operator`) — answered by a reply kind carrying that box's material: its grade, its name, the address it dials, the trust anchor, its leaf and that leaf's key. The seat sends the act over an entry it already holds at operator grade, and shows the answer as a QR symbol the operator points a camera at.

The seat gains a verb (`lernie enroll <entry> <name> --grade foot|operator`) and a window control on an entry.

## Why this is not the in-channel bootstrap §1.4 forbids

Worth settling before any of it is built, because it looks exactly like the thing that must never exist.

REMOTE §1.4 forbids a box acquiring its identity **over its own channel** — no pairing, no first-connect ceremony, no button that hands an unauthenticated dialer a certificate. Nothing here does that. The act crosses a channel that is **already** authenticated, at **operator** grade, on material an operator carried by hand; the box being enrolled is not a party to it and has no channel yet. What reaches the new box reaches it out of channel, through a camera pointed at a screen — the same class of act as carrying a file on a stick, and mediated by the same operator.

The seat still **never mints**: the mint is the engine's, on the engine's box, over a trust root that already exists. The seat asks and paints.

## The DESIGN amendment this needs

`docs/DESIGN.md` §3 ("What lernie may never become") currently says, under *It never mints*:

> Certificates arrive out of channel, by the operator's hand (REMOTE §1.4). There is no enrollment, pairing or bootstrap flow in the channel, ever.

Two sentences, and only the first is the invariant. The second describes the surface as it stood and is what this work changes — an enroll ACT crosses the channel, while the material it produces still reaches its box out of one. Amend it so the section says what may never happen (a box acquiring identity over its own channel) rather than what did not yet exist. Do not code around it; the doc is the architecture authority.

## The material is displayed and then gone

The seat never writes it down. Not to its own state, not to a cache, not to a temporary file, not to a log line. It is held while a symbol is on screen and dropped with the pane.

This is the one moment a seat holds a private key that is not its own, for a box that does not exist yet, and a seat is not a certificate authority's spool. Anything persisted is a credential at rest that nobody chose to store and nobody will remember to delete.

**Test it as an absence.** Drive the act against the stand-in engine over a throwaway root and assert that no file appears anywhere under it — the assertion is over the tree, not over the paths the code happens to know about, because a defect here is precisely a path nobody thought of.

## The encoder is already built, and it lands in this ball's commit

bl-ad61 is the QR encoder — `src/qr/`, byte mode, correction level M, the smallest version that fits, no new dependency. It is **written, at 100% coverage, and proven against an independent implementation** module for module; its body carries the rulings and the two places two reference implementations disagree with each other.

It is **not on `main`**, and cannot be: `warnings = "deny"` refuses a module no production line calls, correctly, and there is no lawful local override. The work is **committed on bl-ad61's own claim worktree**, gate-clean on every step but that one.

So whoever claims this ball resumes bl-ad61 under its original identity, folds that worktree into this one, and closes both together. The encoder needs no further work — it needs the line that calls it, which is this ball.

The remaining unknown is the **encoding** of the envelope — whether the compact JSON is carried raw or compressed. That is yog's measurement, recorded there, not decided here. For scale: a measured enrollment envelope with EC P-256 material is on the order of 1.5 kB raw, which is a mid-thirties version and comfortably inside the encoder's 2331-byte ceiling.
