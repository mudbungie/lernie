## Scope
Design ball for the missing keystone verb (ARCH §6): `lernie advance` — the fresh-subprocess driver that reads the repo, evaluates workflow rules against on-disk state, runs the next action, and execs its successor. Every launcher seam that is currently a documented no-op (`lernie message` probe-and-launch, scan inbox flush — §2.11 shipped-state notes) lands its spawn once this exists. Deliverable is a living design: amendments to ARCHITECTURE.md §6/§2.11 (edited like code), then implementation.

## Must-pin items

**1. Lease continuity across the exec baton (CLOEXEC).** §2.11: 'the lock fd is deliberately inherited across the §6 exec baton.' Rust opens fds O_CLOEXEC by default, so this does NOT happen for free — implemented naively, every advance→advance exec drops and reacquires the flock, opening the exact gap the inheritance clause closes. Fix is the standard fd-passing pattern (systemd socket-activation shape): before exec, `fcntl(fd, F_SETFD, 0)` on the lock fd and publish its number as `LERNIE_LOCK_FD`; the successor adopts that fd as its lease instead of reacquiring and validates it by fstat-comparing against the inbox directory. The flock lease lives on the open file description, which survives exec — no gap.

**2. Launch detachment.** A driver spawned by `lernie message` (possibly from inside another agent's tool subprocess, or a user's script) must outlive its launcher: setsid at spawn, stdio to log/null, own pgid (consistent with bl-535d retiring cross-agent pgid cascade). Pin this in §2.11/§6 — it is the difference between scriptable and flaky-under-scripting.

**3. Re-entry contract.** Acquire-or-exit per Writer/driver totality (§2.11); idempotent procedures per §6; no startup scan on the hot path (see the scan-architecture ball — advance should land against the segmented model, not the current every-invocation scan).
