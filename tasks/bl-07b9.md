`lernie enroll <workspace> <name> <grade>` builds the gesture and dials with no
reading of `grade` at all, so a typo is answered by the engine after a full
round trip:

    $ lernie enroll <a wall> <a name> notagrade
    unknown grade "notagrade"
    $ lernie enroll <a wall> <a name> OPERATOR
    unknown grade "OPERATOR"

Both are true and neither is useful. **The set is closed, it is small, and this
binary already holds it** — `lernie help enroll` says so in its own words:

    `grade` is `operator` or `foot`.

So the seat spends a connection to be told something it could have said, and
the sentence it gets back names neither of the two words that would have
worked. The capitalised case is the sharp one: an operator who knows the
vocabulary and typed it in the wrong case is told the word is unknown, with no
list to compare against.

This is `crate::cli`'s own rule elsewhere. The module doc for `ask`:

> **Read here**, in the pure function, because whether a body is a gesture is
> decided entirely by what was typed — so it is the caller's typo, it earns the
> usage, and it costs no connection.

A grade is decided entirely by what was typed. It is the caller's typo. It
should earn the usage and cost no connection.

**What is NOT wanted: a second authority on grades.** The engine stays the
place that decides what a grade means and whether this box may ask for one
(REMOTE §8.4 — the act is refused unless this box's own leaf is
operator-grade, and that is not knowable here). What the seat can settle is
whether the word is one of the two the boundary defines, which is the same
class of reading `crate::envelope::parse` already does for `op`.

Found by walking `enroll`'s refusals against the 0.1.0 binary. The
absent-workspace and empty-name refusals both behave well and are unaffected.
