`crate::ui::enroll`'s module doc states why this one pane covers another:

> The shell's rule is *a bar rather than a modal*, because a refusal about one
> pane must not stop the operator reading the other three. This is the
> exception and it earns it: what the pane holds is a **private key on a
> screen**, the whole act is "look at this now and then close it", and leaving
> a conversation legible behind it would invite exactly the thing the material
> must not have — a long life on a display. So it replaces the central panel
> while it is open, and the control that closes it is the one that forgets.

It replaces the central panel. It is still not a modal, in the two ways that
matter to that argument.

**Escape does nothing to it.** `crate::ui::keys::handle` routes Escape to
`Model::dismiss`, and `dismiss` is three lines that clear the NOTICE and
nothing else. Driven headless: with the enrollment open and no text box
focused, two Escape presses left the pane exactly as it was — heading, the
name box, the `operator`/`foot` toggle, `mint` and `done — forget it`, all
standing. The only exits are a click on `done — forget it` or a Tab walk to it.
Escape is the key an operator reaches for to close a thing that covers the
window, and for the one pane whose stated purpose is *close it quickly* it is
inert. Under yog's QUALITY F1 (*everything keyboard-operable*) the close is
reachable, which is not the same as operable.

**The composer stands under it, live.** `crate::ui::shell::render` paints the
composer as a `TopBottomPanel::bottom` BEFORE the `CentralPanel` the enrollment
replaces, so it is outside what the enrollment covers. Captured with the
enrollment open on an aimed wall: the start composer paints beneath it, hint
text and a live `start` button, the whole width of the pane. So the window
does leave something legible beside the material, and worse than legible — a
control that fires a conversation on the wall being enrolled into, from under
the symbol.

Both are the same gap: the enrollment covers a PANEL when the argument it is
made from is about the WINDOW. Either it covers everything but its own
controls, or the doc's reason is narrower than it says.

Found by driving the window headless (X server, screenshots read back). No
material was minted — the pane was opened, read and closed with the name box
empty and `mint` disabled.
