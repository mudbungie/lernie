Ten `control`-classed ops with no interactable in this seat: `fleet`, `disband`,
`arm`, `disarm`, `scan`, `science`, `work-diff`, `ops`, `ack`, `clear-trail`.

Two halves of one absence. The fleet verbs are how a workspace is made to run
its ready balls and made to stop, and how the cheap-model watch over it is
raised and dropped; the trail verbs are the record of every act that crossed
the boundary, its alarms, and the truncation that acknowledges them.

## What this ball landed: the trail's READ half

`ops` is painted. `src/reply/ops.rs` decodes the row, `src/verbs/trail.rs`
holds the door (a door and not a table row, because the bound is a number),
`src/ui/trail.rs` is the pane and `src/ui/model/trail.rs` is what it holds
between frames. DESIGN §4.27 is the section.

It is §4.19's shape one noun over: `ops` names no workspace, so the read fans
over every channel and the pane is the union, its control hangs off the roster,
and no aim or selection can invalidate it. It parts from §4.21's other two
channel-wide panes in one place — the read STANDS while the pane is open, where
a verb table and a search are posted once — because a trail is what is
happening.

The seat classifies nothing: REMOTE §9.17 put `failed`, `exit_label` and
`standing` on the wire so no client would re-derive them, so the pane paints
the engine's words and never reads the exit integer.
